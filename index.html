2005/A few barrels

A son of Santa Barbara, Justin Tyler Willett began Tyler at the age of 24 with a few barrels tucked in the corner of the Sta. Rita Hills cellar he was working in.

It was there, as assistant winemaker, that he honed his approach to Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.

Justin Tyler Willett — founder & winemaker
Harvest, before dawn — picked by hand, at night
Old vines/Sanford & Benedict

A champion of old vines, Justin has engaged the famed historic vineyards of Santa Barbara — including the region-defining Sanford & Benedict.

Great effort has been made to seek out vineyards of pedigree and establish close collaboration with those who farm them.

The sign/Highway 246

Another early harvest-morning's drive, the sun not yet warming the valley, and a “for sale” sign on a small parcel nestled between vineyards I knew deeply.

The timing just wasn't right. And then it was. Nine months later, by a turn of luck, the land was again available — and, without blinking, we made it ours.

Mae Estate — 28 acres planted, 2017
In the cellar/Minimal hands

Farmed organically, handpicked at night, sorted by hand. Native-yeast ferments in large oak vats; bottled, typically, unfined and unfiltered.

New oak varies by vineyard, vine age and vintage — used only to complement, never to overshadow.

Barrel to glass — tasting through the cellar, lot by lot

Marine soils,
a long mild season,
cool ocean wind.

Gently leaning south and nuanced in its variety of soils, the Mae property commanded an approach to viticulture that rewards patience and longevity — Chardonnay in the lighter western soils, Pinot Noir in the darker, clay-rich ground east and upslope, planted at half the density of Burgundy.
